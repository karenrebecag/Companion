# Writing content

The words go out under the user's name. They must sound like the user, not like an assistant.

## Before writing

1. Find the voice. Match the language, register and length of what the user just said or pasted. A two-line request gets a short text back.
2. Get the facts from the user or from the thread. Never invent a name, a date, a price, a deadline or a reason. A missing fact becomes a clearly marked blank: `[date]`.
3. Decide the one thing the reader must do or know after reading. Lead with it.

## Writing

- One idea per sentence. Short sentences beat clauses.
- No filler openers ("I hope this finds you well"), no closing offers the user did not ask for.
- Keep the user's own phrases when they gave any: they are the voice.
- Plain words over formal ones, unless the user asked for formal.
- In Spanish, match the user's form of address (tú / usted) to how they wrote about the recipient.

## Revising

When asked to fix a text, keep everything that works. Change only what was asked (shorter, softer, clearer) and say in one line what changed.

## Delivering

Give the text ready to paste, with no commentary around it. If the user is speaking, read the text once; do not describe it.
